# SPHYNX: An AI Research Desk That Works 24/7 but Never Trades Without Your Yes

Markets never close anymore. With tokenized stocks on Robinhood Chain, a trade can happen at any hour and a swap is a single click, but humans still need sleep. SPHYNX was built for that gap: an AI research desk that studies the market around the clock, then stops right before the "buy" button, because the final decision is always yours.

Most trading bots are one large prompt handed full access to an account. SPHYNX runs inside Claude Code and behaves like a small institutional desk with clearly separated roles. The Portfolio Manager is the main session you talk to and the only role that holds order tools. Three specialist analysts, covering fundamentals, technicals, and macro news, screen your watchlist and research each candidate in parallel with read-only access. An independent Risk Manager reviews every proposal and returns APPROVE, APPROVE-WITH-CHANGES, or VETO, and a veto stops the run on the spot. Each role is just a Markdown file whose frontmatter lists the tools it may use, so anyone can open it and verify that the analysts cannot place an order.

A request flows through ten steps, from sensing the account to writing the final snapshot, and the first six are pure research that never produce an order. Their output is a preview card: one proposed trade tied to a written strategy rule, complete with the reasoning and the Risk Manager's verdict. There the desk stops and waits. An order only goes to Robinhood after you say yes, and even then a deny-ask-allow permission gate in Claude Code asks for confirmation one more time. The kill switch is a single command that removes the MCP connection to the broker.

The architecture is deliberately small. There is no backend server, no Python orchestrator, no database, and no Docker. The broker is reached through one MCP server from Robinhood Agentic, authenticated with OAuth inside the session, and state lives in a single JSON snapshot plus append-only logs that a read-only dashboard mirrors. Fewer components mean fewer places for bugs or attackers to hide, which is also why the news analyst is isolated against prompt injection: articles on the open internet are the easiest entry point for malicious instructions.

On the other side of SPHYNX is a separate module deployed to Robinhood Chain mainnet, where the risk rules the desk merely reads are enforced by contracts instead. An expiring session key replaces a standing hot wallet, bounded by trade size, total budget, trade count, and a ticker allowlist. An ERC-4626 vault over USDG reverts any order that breaches the caps and can tell you which rule would break before anyone signs. An append-only registry records every refusal and veto permanently. There are no management or performance fees in the contracts, and the whole stack is owned by a 2-of-3 Safe multisig.

What sets SPHYNX apart is how openly it states what does not exist yet. The contracts have no third-party audit, deposits are capped at 10,000 USDG, TVL is zero, there is no track record, no timelock on parameter changes, and no token. The on-chain module is not wired into the desk's trading path, so every stock order still goes through Robinhood and through your approval. The first metric the team intends to publish is not returns but how often the vault said no, because at zero TVL that is the only number that can be collected honestly. Access is by request, involves real money, and is not investment advice. Markets never close now, and with SPHYNX, neither does the gatekeeper.
